Getting started

Create your account and get to know the app in 2 minutes.

1

Create an account or try the demo

Head to the home page. Click Get started for free to create an account with your email, or Try the demo to jump straight into a pre-filled version with no sign-up.

2

Sign in with Google

From the sign-in window, click Continue with Google to authenticate in one click with your existing Google account.

3

Find your way around

The left sidebar takes you to the Dashboard, Tasks, Calendar, Habits, OKRs, Statistics and your company space. Collapse it with the [ key to give your content more room. Ctrl+K (⌘K on Mac) opens the command palette, and ? lists every keyboard shortcut.

Demo mode is ideal for exploring every feature with realistic data before creating your real account. Demo data is not carried over into your account.

Dashboard

An overview of everything you're working on

Inbox

Shared tasks and lists, contact requests, company invitations

Settings

Theme, account, data, shortcuts

Tasks

Organize, prioritize and keep track of everything on your plate.

1

Create a task

Click New task at the top of the page. Only the name is required; due date, category, priority, duration and lists are all set in the same panel. Even faster: press N from any page and type the task as a single sentence.

2

Set the priority

Every task gets a priority from 1 (highest) to 5 (lowest). Priority 1 shows in red, priority 2 in orange. Sort or filter by priority to focus on what really matters.

3

Mark it done

Tick the circle to the left of the task, or on a phone swipe it to the right. It leaves the active view; find it again with the Done filter. A recurring task creates its next occurrence the moment you tick it.

Bookmarks

Bookmark an important task: it gets a gold highlight and shows up in the Bookmarked filter.

Duration

Set a duration for each task. The Dashboard then shows your total workload.

Filters and search

Bookmarked, Done, Overdue and Shared combine with each other, as do categories, the priority range and the scope (All, Personal, Company). The / key opens search.

Board view

Switch from List to Board: four columns (To do, In progress, Blocked, Done) to drag your tasks between.

Subtasks

Break a task that's too big into steps you tick off. A subtask that grows can be turned into a task of its own.

Dependencies

Mark a task as waiting on one or more others: it then shows how many tasks are still blocking it.

Recurrence

A task can repeat every day, every week or every month.

Collaboration

Share a task with one of your contacts from the Share tab of its panel. It lands in their inbox, then they can follow and complete it from their own account.

Categories

Attach each task to a colored category (Work, Personal, Health...) to see where your time goes.

Quick add (N) understands the date and four symbols: #health for the category, !! for the priority (!!! = 1, !! = 2, ! = 3), ~30m for the duration. The date words are French for now: “Appeler le dentiste jeudi #santé !! ~30m”. Enter creates the task and keeps the field open for the next one.

Lists

Group your tasks by project or context for effortless organization.

1

Create a list

At the top of the Tasks page, in the lists bar, click New list. Give it a name and a color. The list shows up right away as a new chip next to the others.

2

Add tasks to a list

In a task's panel, pick one or more lists. The same task can belong to several lists at once, for instance a client and “This week”.

3

Filter by list

Click a list in the bar to show only the tasks it contains. The counter next to the name shows how many active tasks it holds.

Smart lists

Today, Overdue, This week, High priority: their content updates by itself, with nothing to file by hand.

Default list

Pin a list so it opens every time you land on the Tasks page.

Share a list

Share a whole list with a contact: it arrives in their inbox, with all its tasks.

List color

Give each list a distinctive color so you can tell them apart at a glance.

Use lists to separate your projects: a Work list, a Personal list, one per client or per active project. It keeps different contexts from blurring together in a single view.

Calendar

Plan your events and take in your whole week at a glance.

1

Create an event

Click a time slot in the grid, or the New button. Set the title, date, start and end time, and category.

2

Schedule a task

Open the Tasks panel and drag a task onto a slot: the event is created with its duration and category, and stays linked to the task. On a phone, tap the task to pick its slot.

3

Switch views

Toggle between Day, Week and Month with the buttons at the top. The Week view strikes the best balance between detail and overview.

Drag & drop

Drag any event to reschedule it in seconds, without opening a form.

Recurring events

Create a daily or weekly event once, it repeats on its own. The Recurring button gathers them all.

Color categories

Each category has its own color. Your calendar becomes a visual dashboard of your time.

Finished slot

Once a task's slot has passed, Cosmo asks whether it got done: you complete it, postpone it or delete it.

Block time in your calendar for your important tasks (time blocking). It prevents overload and makes your days more predictable.

An event is not a task: the task is the action to tick off, the event is when you do it. Dragging a task into the calendar links the two, and confirming the slot ticks the task.

Habits

Build routines that last and watch your progress day after day.

1

Create a habit

Click New habit. Give it a name, a duration, a color and, if you like, a category. The habit appears in the tracking board.

2

Tick it off daily

Each day, tick the habits you've done by clicking the matching cell in the table: your streak goes up. A habit's menu also lets you schedule it in the calendar or turn it into a task for today.

3

Switch views

Toggle between Table, List and Overall tracking with the tabs at the top of the page to match the view to what you want to analyze.

Streak

The orange counter shows how many days in a row you've done the habit. The streak resets to zero if you miss a day.

Completion rate

Shown in your statistics, it measures the share of days you ticked the habit off over the selected period.

Pause

Vacation, illness: pause a habit, for a set time or indefinitely. Your streak stays intact until you resume.

Weekly recap

The Recap button generates an image of your week, ready to share. No task or habit name appears on it.

Start with 2 to 3 habits at most. It's far easier to build on small regular wins than to burn out on a long list from day one.

OKRs: objectives and key results

Set ambitious objectives and measure your progress with concrete indicators.

What is an OKR?

O for Objective

A qualitative, inspiring ambition. E.g. "Accelerate product growth"

KR for Key Result

A measurable indicator that proves the objective is met. E.g. "Reach 10,000 active users"

1

Create an objective

Click New objective. Give it a title, a category and a due date (often the end of the quarter). The description is optional.

2

Add key results

In the same window, add at least one key result with its target value and unit. Three per objective is good practice. Under More options, a weight from 1 to 10 gives a key result more say in the overall progress.

3

Update your progress

Move a key result forward with the + and − buttons on its card. The objective's progress is computed automatically, weights included. A key result that hits its target is marked Reached.

Time elapsed

Each card shows the share of time elapsed next to your progress: if progress moves faster than the clock, you're ahead.

Weekly check-in

A quick pass over your key results, one at a time. It also opens by itself at the start of the week from the Dashboard.

Linked tasks and slots

From a key result, create a task or schedule an event: they stay attached to it, and the card adds up the time spent.

Completed OKRs

A completed objective moves to the Completed OKRs list, where you can still view or edit it.

Categories

Sort your OKRs by category (Product, Personal, Team) to keep things organized.

A good key result is binary or measurable: either you can put a number on it (e.g. 10,000 users), or it's met or it isn't. Avoid vague wording like "Improve quality".

Statistics

Analyze your productivity and spot where you can improve.

1

Pick a section and a period

At the top of the page, choose the section to analyze (Overview, Tasks, Calendar, OKRs, Habits) then the period: Day, Week, Month or Year. Every chart updates automatically.

2

Read the charts

The page opens with a plain-language summary, followed by time invested per period compared with the previous one, then detailed charts for the section. Hover the bars to see the details.

3

Spot the trends

Compare week after week. A steady decline in one area is a signal to adjust your objectives or your habits.

Tasks

Number of tasks completed per period

Calendar

Time spent on scheduled events

OKRs

Progress gained during the period, objective by objective

Habits

Average completion rate of your active habits

Statistics are computed in real time from your own data. The more regularly you use Cosmo, the more the charts reflect your real work habits.

Company mode

A full collaboration space: teams, shared projects, tasks and OKRs, custom roles and fine-grained per-member permissions.

1

Create or join a company

In the sidebar, click Create or join. Give your organization a name to create it, or enter the company code a colleague sent you to join theirs.

2

Invite members

Invite one of your contacts directly: the invitation lands in their inbox. You can also share an invite link or the company code; a colleague who enters the code sends a request, which an admin approves.

3

Find your way around

Sections fall into three groups: My work (Overview, Tasks), Steering (Projects, OKRs) and Organization (Members, Teams, Settings).

Roles

Two statuses: Admin, who runs the organization, and Member. An admin then creates custom roles, each a named set of rights, granted with no end date or for a chosen period, and picks the role given automatically to new members.

Member permissions

In Settings › Roles and permissions, an admin sets what a member can do by default. From the directory, “Edit permissions” then adjusts one person's rights one by one: tasks, projects, OKRs, categories, teams, invitations, reports and roles.

Teams

Cross-functional teams, each with a lead who manages its members and projects without being a company admin.

Team projects

Create shared projects, assign them to members, and archive them once done. A project's tasks stay visible to the whole team that has access to it.

Team tasks

Assign a task to one or more members, set dependencies between tasks (one can't start before another is done), and track their progress together.

Task approval

When you create a task, pick an approver among the members. If someone else completes it, it moves to “To approve”, and only the approver closes it.

Team OKRs

Shared objectives, closed by default: you open them to the whole company, to certain teams, or to a chosen list of people. The ones you contribute to also appear on your OKR page.

Activity reports

In beta, from Overview or Settings: the company report is reserved for admins by default, and a team lead reads their own team's.

Invitations

Four ways to bring a member in: invite them from your contacts or by email, share an invite link, or give them the company code (with admin approval). A departure goes through an assistant that reassigns their tasks and responsibilities.

First set the members' default rights in Settings › Roles and permissions, then create a role for each group that needs more: an individual exception should stay the exception.

Up to 5 members, company mode is free. Beyond that, you pick a subscription by member tier, monthly or yearly (25% less), prices including VAT. The owner manages it in Settings › Plan and billing.

The Cosmo user guide

This guide covers the six areas of Cosmo, in the order you meet them in practice. Allow around fifteen minutes to read it all, but nothing obliges you to set everything up on day one: tasks alone are enough to start usefully.

Getting started

There are two ways in. Demo mode opens the complete application with no sign-up, pre-filled with 12 months of data: that is the right way to explore without building anything. A free account takes thirty seconds to create, by email or through Google, and your data then follows you from one device to another.

Tasks

The foundation. A task carries a name, a description, a priority from 1 to 5, a coloured category, a deadline and one or more lists. Filters combine to isolate what matters now, and lists serve lasting groupings (a client, a project, a course). A recurring task regenerates itself automatically when you tick it, and subtasks break down whatever is too big to start. Sharing is done by email, as Viewer or Editor, free of charge.

Habits

Create the habit, give it a duration, then tick it off as the days go by. A pause (holiday, illness) keeps your streak intact. The 26-week heatmap shows your real consistency, the streak counts consecutive days, and the completion rate situates the current period. The advice that changes everything: two or three habits at most when you start, even if you add more in a month. A list of fifteen habits almost always ends in giving up on all of them.

Calendar

The calendar accepts ordinary events, but its value lies elsewhere: drag a task from the side panel onto a slot and the event is created and stays linked to the task. That is time-blocking, and it is what turns a list of intentions into a realistic week. Day, week and month views, with recurring events managed from the same panel.

OKRs

One qualitative objective, 2 to 5 numeric key results. You update the current value of a key result, Cosmo recomputes the objective's progress and records each completion in your history, and that trace is what feeds the dashboard chart. If the method is new to you, the article the OKR method with 15 examples gives you wordings ready to adapt.

Statistics

Time invested per category, how it evolves over the chosen period, comparison between modules. This is the page to open at the end of a week or a month: it answers "where did my time go?" with numbers rather than an impression, and that is often where the next adjustment gets decided.
